> Agreed

sound rude

but if you're a fresher or recent CS grad and really want to get ahead in this market, don't be average at learning.

everyone is learning web dev, AI wrappers, the same frameworks, the same projects.

pick something that most people aren't bothering to understand deeply.

##### could be Python's GIL and internals, C++ performance/optimization, compilers, CUDA, distributed systems, databases/storage engines, networking, runtimes, ML inference, browser engines

pick one.

go deep. then go broad around it.

spend months becoming genuinely good at it.

i also did this

got deeper into C++, then Python bindings, then ML inference, and eventually ended up contributing to CERN's framework

the interesting thing is, there are some seriously big projects out there that are openly built by the community and actually need people who understand the problem well.

not AI generated 500 line PRs.

actual, meaningful contributions.

so if you don't know where to start

pick something hard, slightly weird, and worth getting really good at.

go ahead and FAFO.

you might be surprised where it takes you.
