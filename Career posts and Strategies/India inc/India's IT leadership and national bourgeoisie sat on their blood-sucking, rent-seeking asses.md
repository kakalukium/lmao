It was not always like this. We had a chance. India had a decade-long window from around 2005 to 2015 when we could have still done something. 

Cloud computing and Big Data were the big buzzwords back then. AI/ML was still nascent. But many knew which way the wind (and capital) would soon start blowing. It was inevitable.

But India's IT leadership and national bourgeoisie sat on their blood-sucking, rent-seeking asses, interested in little more than leeching more and more wealth. 

We had everything we needed. All the ingredients were staring us squarely in the face:
- Endless bourgeois wealth
- The wealthiest diaspora in the western world 
- Countless talented and experienced professionals
- Decades of institutional expertise
- Strong and deep global IT contracts
- Privileged access to global IT markets 
- Strong partnerships with western IT consultants and resellers
- Ubiquitous English fluency 
- A billion people's data
- Even favorable geopolitical PR in the West 

Takes a country like India to screw up an opportunity this tailor-made for its strengths. Objectively one of the greatest squandered advantages in the history of modern India.
