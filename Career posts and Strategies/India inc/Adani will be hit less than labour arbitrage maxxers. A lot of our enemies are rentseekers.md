⚡️The real event is the collapse of the white-collar apprenticeship economy.

The junior analyst, associate, consultant, paralegal, accountant, recruiter, researcher, coder, finance professional, and coordinator were all expensive because competent cognition was scarce. AI is erasing that scarcity.

Within five years, a huge share of that work will simply stop being purchased from humans. The exact corporate mechanism will look deceptively boring: smaller incoming classes, vacancies left open, fewer backfills, one senior person running what used to require five juniors.

So the labor statistics will lag the reality.

The more important break comes afterward.

Those junior jobs were how people became senior people.

A first-year lawyer reviewed documents and drafted language until pattern recognition formed.

A banking analyst built models and decks until transactions became intuitive.

A finance analyst reconciled numbers until the business became legible.

A consultant researched industries until judgment developed.

AI consumes the training substrate.

That means the career ladder loses its bottom rungs.

Then firms start hiring fewer people at the bottom while expecting the survivors to operate much closer to senior level immediately. The funnel gets brutally narrow.

That creates a strange second-order shortage later: AI can replace junior production faster than institutions can replace human judgment formed through years of apprenticeship.

So the professional class bifurcates.

At one end: a small number of humans directing enormous machine capacity, owning decisions, relationships, capital, trust, and accountability.

At the other: millions of educated people discovering that being smart, diligent, credentialed, and competent no longer guarantees economic scarcity.

That is the real earthquake.

And it reaches universities next.

A $150,000 degree made sense when it purchased entry into a protected cognitive labor market. Once the entry jobs disappear, the degree loses part of the asset it was selling.

Then wages.

Then urban professional economies.

Then commercial real estate.

Then tax bases.

Then family formation.

Then politics.

The shock propagates far beyond employment.

The deepest shift is simpler:

For most of modern history, human cognition was capital.

A company hired people because useful thinking could only be obtained by hiring minds.

Now useful thinking can increasingly be rented by the token.

That changes the economic status of intelligence itself.

Once intelligence becomes infrastructure, the premium moves toward what remains scarce after intelligence becomes abundant:

judgment

ownership

trust

capital allocation

relationships

taste

responsibility

access

the ability to choose the right objective

The professional middle class was built on the scarcity of competent cognition.

AI is removing the scarcity beneath the class.

That is what is actually happening.
