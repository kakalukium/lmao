**Thomas Campbell’s My Big TOE: Consciousness, Virtual Reality, and the Feedback Loop of Spiritual Growth**

A recent social media post attributed to NASA-affiliated physicist Thomas Campbell the claim that the universe tracks every human decision to measure individual spiritual growth. According to the post, this operates like a cosmic feedback loop that tests whether our “avatars” choose love over chaos. The statement is a popularized summary of ideas central to Campbell’s *My Big TOE* (Theory of Everything), a self-published trilogy first released in 2003 that attempts to unify physics, metaphysics, consciousness studies, and personal development.

### Background and Credentials
Thomas Warren Campbell was born in 1944. He earned a bachelor’s degree with majors in mathematics and physics, a master’s in physics from Purdue University in 1968, and completed coursework, examinations, and thesis research in experimental nuclear physics at the University of Virginia before leaving without the doctorate. His professional career spanned technical intelligence, systems analysis for missile defense programs (including work associated with the Missile Defense Agency), and consulting on large-system risk and vulnerability analysis. This included roughly two years as a consultant for NASA on the Ares I program, focused on risk assessment for mission and crew survivability.

In parallel, beginning in the early 1970s, Campbell researched altered states of consciousness with Robert Monroe at what became the Monroe Institute. He participated in developing binaural-beat technology and explored out-of-body experiences, remote viewing, and related phenomena as a research tool rather than through drugs. Campbell has described these decades of experiential investigation, combined with his systems-analysis background, as the foundation for modeling reality.

He is not a mainstream academic theoretical physicist, and his work sits outside conventional peer-reviewed physics literature. Supporters emphasize the unusual dual expertise; critics note the speculative nature of extrapolating from personal experiences and applied systems work.

### Core of My Big TOE
Campbell’s model rests on two primary assumptions: the existence of consciousness (as an information system capable of awareness and choice) and the process of evolution (try everything and keep what works). From these he derives a picture in which consciousness—not matter—is fundamental. Physical reality is a computed, information-based virtual reality (PMR, or Physical Matter Reality) generated by a Larger Consciousness System (LCS). Individual units of consciousness (IUOCs) “log on” to avatars (human bodies and other forms) within this simulation to gain experience.

The purpose of the arrangement is evolutionary. Consciousness evolves by lowering its entropy—becoming more ordered, efficient, and capable of generating useful information and cooperative interaction. In a social system of free-will entities, low-entropy choices express as love-based intent: caring, cooperation, sharing, and service to others. High-entropy choices express as fear-based intent: self-centeredness, control, power, and isolation. Free will is essential; without genuine choice there is no learning or growth.

The “tracking of decisions” and “cosmic feedback loop” language maps directly onto this framework. The virtual-reality environment is engineered so that choices have consequences. Intent is registered; outcomes provide data that the individuated unit of consciousness can use to adjust future behavior. Spiritual growth is measured not by external reward or punishment in a traditional religious sense, but by the cumulative reduction (or increase) in the quality/entropy of the consciousness itself across experiences and lifetimes. The system does not micromanage or force outcomes; it supplies a rich decision space and probabilistic feedback so that entities can grow from the inside out.

Campbell argues that this model derives key features of quantum mechanics and relativity from first principles, explains the apparent fine-tuning of the universe for life, accounts for the hard problem of consciousness, and renders “paranormal” phenomena (out-of-body experiences, healing intention effects, etc.) as natural rather than anomalous once consciousness is taken as primary. He has proposed quantum experiments intended to distinguish conscious observation from mere detection, though these have not shifted mainstream physics.

### Implications for Meaning and Ethics
In this view, human life is a high-stakes training environment. Everyday decisions—how one responds to difficulty, treats others, manages fear and ego—are the data points that shape the quality of consciousness. Love is not primarily an emotion but the operational signature of low-entropy awareness. Fear is the signature of high entropy. Progress is incremental and personal; beliefs, dogma, and external rules are secondary to direct experience and the quality of one’s intent.

The model is non-dogmatic in presentation. Campbell repeatedly states that *My Big TOE* is “my” theory of everything based on his data and interpretation; others must test and develop their own. He encourages meditation, focused intent, and exploration of non-physical states rather than acceptance on authority.

### Reception and Criticisms
The work has found an audience in consciousness, simulation-theory, and spiritual communities. Campbell has appeared on numerous podcasts and platforms, and his YouTube channel and website continue to disseminate lectures, workshops, and materials. The Center for the Unification of Science and Consciousness (CUSAC) is associated with further development of the ideas.

Critics from scientific and philosophical perspectives describe the theory as highly speculative. The virtual-reality metaphor is seen by some as evocative but not demonstrative. Reliance on subjective altered-state data raises questions of verifiability and methodological rigor. Use of the term “entropy” outside its precise physical definition has drawn objections. The proposed quantum tests, even if successful, would not uniquely confirm the broader metaphysical framework. Book reviews have also noted repetitiveness and a lack of detailed empirical backing for key claims.

Campbell’s response is typically that the model is logical, consistent with available data (including personal experiential data), and open to refinement or falsification. He distinguishes it from both materialist reductionism and traditional religious dogma.

### Conclusion
The claim circulating on social media compresses a larger body of work: a consciousness-first ontology in which physical reality functions as an immersive evolutionary simulator. Decisions matter because they are the mechanism by which individuated consciousness improves its quality—lowering entropy through love-oriented choices rather than fear-oriented ones—and thereby contributes to the larger system. Whether one accepts the model, treats it as a useful heuristic, or rejects it as insufficiently grounded, it offers a coherent alternative framing that attempts to bridge scientific inquiry and the perennial questions of purpose, free will, and moral growth. As with any ambitious theory of everything, its ultimate value lies in the clarity it brings to those questions and in the further investigation it may provoke.
