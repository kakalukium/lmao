Once you understand money, sex, and interests, you also understand the practical logic behind love and marriage. Money is the most practical foundation of a relationship: it determines whether two people can share the pressures of daily life, and it also affects how secure each person feels and the choices they make within the relationship. Sex is about attraction and desire in an intimate relationship. Many relationships begin with physical attraction, and they can also gradually grow cold as desire fades. But beneath money and sex lies something deeper: interests. Whether in a romantic relationship or a marriage, a long-term partnership inevitably involves an exchange of value. Some people need financial support, others need emotional companionship, and some value resources and a future together. A relationship is easier to sustain when both partners’ needs continue to be met. This does not mean genuine love does not exist; rather, even genuine love cannot exist independently of reality. You can believe in love, but do not forget that relationships always have to contend with reality. When your needs no longer align, even the deepest feelings may come to an end.
