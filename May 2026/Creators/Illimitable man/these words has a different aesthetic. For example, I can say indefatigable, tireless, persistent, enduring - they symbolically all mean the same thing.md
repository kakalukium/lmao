There's a few reasons for this "different personalities in different languages" effect.

1: English actually has a lot more different words and variations for things. There are entire words/concepts/nuances that exist in English, that simply do not exist in other languages. Especially if your native language is a rural dialect version of said language.

Each of these words has a different aesthetic. For example, I can say: indefatigable, tireless, persistent, enduring - they symbolically all mean the same thing, but they're all slightly different - so there is more granular precision.

If I say I am enduring, you think "deeply affected, but not exhausted or defeated whilst carrying on" - an acutely felt price is paid - indefatigable on the other hand is more imperial - it is to communicate the same thing, but as if you are a superior being who is not viscerally affected. So whilst they say the same thing, they say it in a very different way. Enduring is resilient under pressure, where indefatigable is untouchably persistent under pressure. Persistent itself just means you don't give up, but lacks the connotation of either. You see they are synonyms, but variations on a theme.

2: English is not tied into a poetic/songful register. Yes, you can make English beautiful, but you can also make it judicial or cold. For example, Saxon derived words are more direct, informal and visceral, where French derived words are colder, more abstract, and formal.

If you want to create intimacy of feeling or a sense of kinship in English, you would tend more towards Saxon words. If you wished to create distance and convey an aesthetic of intelligent hyper self-control, you would use French derived words.

Compare both of these paragraphs in English for example, the first uses Saxon derived words where the second one uses French derived words.

Saxon derived:
The old smith stood in the yard to weigh the heavy iron bars. He had to look hard at the thick metal to find any deep flaws or breaks. His hands were rough from long years of hard work, but his mind was sharp. He could size up the whole load by eye alone. He knew the true worth of the smithy, and he felt glad that his folk had built such a strong stead.

French derived:
The senior inspector paused to evaluate the substantial metallic components. It was imperative to examine the durable material to discover any significant defects or fractures. His palms were calloused from decades of intense labour, but his intellect remained acute. He possessed the capacity to appraise the entire cargo purely by sight. He recognized the immense value of the enterprise, and he experienced great joy that his ancestors had constructed such a magnificent establishment.

French derived words use more words to say the same thing whilst concealing the true emotional interiority of the speaker. The Saxon is embodied where the French reads more like a meta analysis.

The final and most crucial point, is your nervous system. You were originally wired by your mother tongue. That is the first language you thought in, were scared in, got hurt in, felt angry/sad/disappointed in, had your formative memories in etc. Words in that language hit you deeper than words in English. You are more vulnerable in that language, because you were a child in that language. Double that up with a language that is innately less scientific and tends more towards baking in honorifics for elders, poeticism, and doesn't have the French derived philological split for creating "cold formal intellectual distance" in everyday speech, and you find yourself more constrained in your expression.

Generally speaking, if English is not someone mother's tongue, when they speak it the personality/behaviour that comes with it is the one they created for themselves by escaping into it through the multitude of self-expression it allows for their native tongue does not, as well as the profound impact this newfound mode of expression had on their self-concept when they began thinking in it.

This is also why your parents who never learned English or who speak it poorly and prefer not to use it, who force you to speak in your mother tongue and still tell you off in it make you feel small and spiritually regress you to something closer to your child self. Even if it is not deliberate, malicious and intentional (and it can be) - it operates as a control mechanism. They know they can control you better in that language, and you know you cannot defend yourself as well in it.
