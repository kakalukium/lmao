Agree.

I will say that when I have thought along similar lines, I have been discouraged from even wanting to make an effort, by the seeming-inevitability of another decline.

‘If we can’t fix things and their root causes — so that they never happen again — forever, then what’s the point?!’

So I think it’s important for any of us thinking along such lines, to make peace with the possibility of a recurrent/cyclical/rhyming history: accept that it’s still meaning-providing and virtue-building and edifying or even our duty, to work to fix things in the here and now, even if we expect things to decline and degenerate yet again in future.

For after all , the only alternative (to making peace with the impermanence of ourncorrections) is nihilism: never trying, being discouraged before even beginning.

Feels like a similar mental motion / habit to:

- ‘Even if I build a comfortable existence, it’ll get frittered away within a generation or two — so what’s the point?!’ (some validity to this tho)

- ‘If I can’t feed all the poor / help all the needy / save every animal / fix [X] problem entirely, I won’t even bother trying to chip away at it.’ (appreciate premises might be uncompelling to you, my Nietzschean friend; but nonetheless this mental motion still exists in some — including myself)

Important for any ideology or movement to a oid this mindset (being perceived) in its ideology or its adherents.
