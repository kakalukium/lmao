If you’re an easy going, emotionally regulated and respectful person, you’ll notice many times in life that people will work a lot harder trying to maintain the connections of those in their life with the complete opposite energy from you. You’ll watch other people be rude, mean, cold, disrespectful, toxic, difficult, inconsistent, emotionally volatile, and exhibit traits that really should not require accommodating or enabling, yet somehow those people receive a level of effort, grace and compassion from others that you rarely ever see directed towards you.

---

You’ll see your loved ones chasing after these people, trying to maintain contact, trying to repair things, trying to keep the connection alive, compromising, apologising, making excuses for them, showing them visible love, appreciation and dedication, and you’ll see them genuinely distraught when those people leave. They’ll be upset that they can no longer have them in their life, they’ll sit there wondering what they could’ve done differently, they’ll fight to keep them, and it can be incredibly confusing to witness because you’re sitting there thinking, “I’m actually good to you. I’m actually kind to you. I’m sweet to you. I don’t speak to you the way these people do. I don’t disrespect you. I don’t make your life difficult. I don’t constantly put you in positions where you have to prove your love to me.

---

And yet, somehow, they receive a version of you that comes with urgency, compassion, care, compromise, sacrifice and visible fear of loss, while the people who show up consistently and treat you well are often treated like they’ll just always be there.”

Although you don’t treat people well for a reward, it can still make you question whether there is any reward in being a good person at all, or whether the world just bends more for people who make themselves difficult to have around.

---

A lot of this comes down to emotional conditioning. People often respond more intensely to inconsistency than stability. Someone who is unpredictable, hot and cold, difficult to please or constantly threatening the connection creates urgency. Their withdrawal feels like a crisis and their approval feels harder to earn so people become more motivated to chase it. Meanwhile, the emotionally regulated person feels safe and dependable. Unfortunately, people can become lazy with what feels secure. It’s not always that the difficult person is more loved. Sometimes they’re just more anxiety provoking and anxiety creates action.

---

That’s why you can watch someone bend over backwards for a person that treats them terribly, then barely move when they’ve hurt somebody who has always treated them well. They don’t feel the same threat of losing you because you haven’t trained them to associate your love with chaos. The toxic person creates consequences immediately. Being easy going can become dangerous if you don’t also have boundaries. Sometimes people only realise how much they valued the peaceful person once that person finally stops being available.

---

And I think that’s why good people have to learn that their goodness should never depend on how much somebody else appreciates it. You have to be a good person because that is who you are, while also being willing to remove yourself from people who mistake your kindness for something they can neglect. The answer isn’t becoming toxic so people chase you. But becoming less accessible to people who only know how to appreciate what makes them anxious.
