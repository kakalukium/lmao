**Ray Peat** viewed **intention** not as a mystical force for manifesting external desires, but as a **biological and physiological process** rooted in the organism's capacity to perceive and adapt to its environment. In his framework, intention arises from an **"intention image"** or mental representation of a desired state (such as satiation or safety) that guides behavior and metabolic action to restore functional integrity. 

Key aspects of Peat’s view include:

- **Intention as Perception:** Intention is not a separate cognitive step but is inherent in the direct perception of a situation. It involves sensing a "gap" between the current depleted state and a more complete one, driving the organism to act. 
    
- **The Orienting Reflex:** Healthy intention is linked to the **orienting reflex**, a systemic response to novelty that allows the organism to explore, assimilate, and integrate new experiences, rather than merely reacting defensively. 
    
- **Metabolic Basis:** The ability to form and pursue intentions depends on **adequate energy** (specifically oxidative metabolism). A well-fueled nervous system supports flexibility, creativity, and the "art of becoming," whereas energy deficiency leads to rigidity and a focus on survival rather than growth.
    
- **Rejection of Scarcity:** Peat critiqued the idea that life is defined by scarcity or competition. Instead, he argued that **abundance** allows for the "growth reflex," where intention is directed toward exploration, connection, and increased complexity rather than mere defense. 
    

Peat did not advocate for manifestation techniques aimed at controlling external outcomes; rather, he emphasized **perceiving complexity** and allowing intentions to emerge from a state of metabolic health and open engagement with the world.
