Immanuel Kant explained this exact reality, noting that most people choose to remain in a state of mental immaturity simply because it is convenient to let others take on the difficult burden of thinking. He explicitly stated, "It is so comfortable to be a minor. If I have a book that thinks for me... I have no need to think."

By letting figures like doctors or teachers command their choices, people inadvertently outsource their own intelligence. This constant reliance reinforces a deep dependency, conditioning the mind to fear the apparent dangers of independent thought.

The result is a lifelong, self-inflicted childhood. They become completely incapable of functioning without a master, permanently relying on external guidance and paralyzed by the fear of making their own decisions.
