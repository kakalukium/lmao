Low wages and red tape.

The McDonald brothers bought a theatre in Hollywood with money saved up from running errands - moving props, driving trucks, and basically asking people if they needed help and being paid to do it.

When the theatre business didn’t take off, they pivoted to food. One McDonald brother was curious how creme biscuits always had the precise amount of creme between them, and wondered if he could use the same contraption to dispense ketchup and mustard sauce on hamburgers.

He posed as a journalist and went up to the biscuit factory. He was warmly welcomed into the premises and shown around. Upon asking his silly question, he was listened to patiently by a representative of the company and furnished a satisfactory explanation. The rep explained how creme was dispensed from a conical funnel with a release trigger. When pulled back, contents would dispense at a steady rate, and the amount of creme could be controlled through timing. He would then take this know-how to a machinist, who would make him the now iconic sauce dispensers.

While no longer made with stainless steel, they are still built from the same principle.

The McDonald brothers did not succeed only through sheer perseverence and grit but the combination of:

1.  Unskilled work that paid enough to buy a fucking movie theatre. Imagine a restaurant waiter in 2026 buying a theatre on Hollywood Boulevard. Impossible!
2.  Generosity baked into the culture. People differentiated on merit and did not tear each other down pursing the last dollar. This flourished a free economy of ideas where anyone who was willing to learn could make dime. The McDonald brothers learnt to make hamburgers from the same people they got the grill from.
3.  No licensing fees or other regulatory hoop-jumping. The brothers operated like lunatics, hopping from one idea to the next with little restraint, entering industries they had no formal experience or connections in. They were free to take risks because the upfront investment was labour and material costs, not a “Food Safety” permit. The brilliant sauce dispenser would probably get their restaurant fined today for “not being food grade!”

If capitalism must work today, you need drastically lower barriers to entry, which means:

1.  Dismantling every monopoly ever. Companies were less consolidated and networked during the time of McDonald brothers than today. No federal grants for Anthropic, no bail-outs for Google, no subsidies for Gadkari. No policy-making that benefits existing businesses - because they are too big to fail and GDP matters more - thus allowing grassroots ingenuity to fill the void left by failed ventures. This will never happen unless the Boomer class dies off, because those who’ve made money do not want to see their savings and stocks go to shit. You cannot have anti-trust laws when politicians and businesses work together to keep each other afloat.
2. Drastically cutting regulation. Let people be the judge of quality (and safety) and vote with their money, not “Approved, OK” stamps from ten different authorities. This allows people to take small risks and gain experience without committing fully with money they don’t have, as required of every business in its formative years. Samsung went from being a grocery store to a tech conglomerate.
3.  Paying livable wages. An entry-level job pays scraps while requiring a Master’s degree and 2+ years of dedicated experience that narrows career options. One must toil many years in exactly the same line of profession before they can expect a decent wage. What if I want to apprentice as a programmer for a month, but I get a bright idea one day and want to move to another venture or gig? Impossible by today’s standards. Let’s not even get to fact that unskilled labour (running errands, gig work) pays next to nothing. Only monomaniacs get paid, the creative mind has nowhere to start.

The McDonald brothers did not have degrees. They did not need to prep for standardised tests, college entrance exams, or internships. They spent more time out on the field - learning, seeing, and refining their craft - than crammed up into a room worrying about the next hoop to jump - about whose letter of recommendation/referral to get or which certificate course to take. And they built arguably the most innovative (at its time) fast food restaurant chain.

Truth be told, capitalism does not work today, and the proof is in the data. Most young folks resort to speculation and trading because those are the only avenues where money can still be made. Why else do you think Polymarket exploded in popularity among US youth or cryptocurrency gambling became a hit among Korean men in their 20s? Every man in his prime today (myself included) is speculating from his bedroom to make money, because the real world has been entirely locked down, snapshotted in time, frozen in order to preserve the monied class. This is an economy eating itself out from within, where speculation has higher payouts than putting in the actual work!

![[images/3c7bf0b751d6f18ac529716b7e1805eb_MD5.webp]]

Others who cannot secure speculative income choose to withdraw entirely from capitalist society. Among Chinese youth, they are called bai lan, hikikomori among Japanese, or NEET (short for not in education, employment, or training) in North America. Withdrawal becomes the rational choice when wages do not even offset the rising costs of shelter, food, and utilities. I distinctly remember from my employment days where I was incurring a net loss because my salary did not cover rent, transportation, food, and utilities that I had to borrow from parents. It was cheaper to just stay at home with my parents.
