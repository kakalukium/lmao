7/ Never do piecemeal negotiations. This is the sign of a bad negotiator. You should always listen to the other party’s requests. You can say, “I might be able to offer X on this point” but I want to understand all of the points first before commenting. It’s always best when you can avoid agreeing things in real time unless you’ve prepared in advance.

9/ In a negotiation you should always write out what you believe is important to the other party. Also, ask them directly what’s important to them. You can then test them by saying “are you saying you’re trying to maximize X or minimize Y?” The more they’re talking about what they want the better your negotiation will go.

10/ Always know if you’re negotiating with the “principal” or the “agent.” The principal is the real decision maker. Even if you *think* the person is empowered they might have to go back and talk with their boss or a committee. You need to understand who the “(wo)man behind the man” is. And what they want. And how to make the agent look good. Don’t be afraid to ask if other people are involved.

11/ Too many people cede control to lawyers in a complex negotiation. They can be there to reduce tension on complex points. But know what is a commercial point vs. a legal point. Never let them take the lead on commercial points. That’s your job. And you don’t want to negotiate these points with their lawyers. You want the company as your interlocutor.
