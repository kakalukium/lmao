

Originally Answered: How does one succeed at life?

I find a startling theme among the good majority of what society would consider successful people, some of which include:

1. Aggressive self-promotion. The greatest lie told in the history of capitalism is that we live in a meritocracy where good ideas take center stage. We live in a false meritocracy - where competence is conflated for tact and usefulness. When you don’t aggressively promote yourself, nobody is going to know what you’re capable of or have in store to offer. Our system doesn’t value subtlety or sophistication, it pursues the obvious and the known. When renowned theoretical physicist Albert Einstein was offered the high rank of the Israeli presidency, he blatantly refused this offer citing his own incompetence in political matters. A true businessman would have made his pitch for the presidency before anyone could offer him that. In other words, you have to put yourself out there, adamantly, and hold yourself to a high esteem. Some of the most successful people aren’t necessarily the most deserving or competent in any true sense of the word. They’re there because only they were egotistical enough to want that amount of power and control over other people.
2. Opportunistic. Successful people lack morals, by necessity. You have to be willing to jump on whatever bandwagon comes riding your way destined for financial and social success. You cannot expect to make a living through your own independent explorations and self-achievements that the masses cannot comprehend. Achievements that no institution can recognise you for. Which brings me to my next point..
3. Appeal to the lowest common denominator. All successful businesses do this. If you want to target and draw in as many segments of the population, you need to market yourself to the layman. You need to deliberately put yourself down to the perceptual level of the average masses. If you want to be successful as an artist, you’re not going to land yourself a record deal should you pursue what you think is worth pursuing. You create a product the masses want - or your collaborate with the industry to condition them to want it - manufacturing consent in the process as Noam Chomsky calls it.
4. Charismatic. Being an extrovert is a massive add-on to your business repertoire. Capitalism runs on sentiment. You are selling a feeling - that buys people a sense of identity and belonging - community - in a rapidly devolving world where fault lines are developing across the fabric of society. When religion, tradition and cultural/racial identity go out the window, the identity from brand association takes over. People buy Apple’s AirPods not because they care about audio quality - surely Apple doesn’t make quality products for what its worth - but rather because it makes them feel good. It makes them part of a broader community of Apple owners. It projects a certain degree of affluence, offering a metaphorical “I’ve made it!” badge to wear proudly on your earlobes. Theranos founder Elizabeth Holmes didn’t make a fortune by selling good ideas, let alone by being competent at her craft - she got investors on-board by selling them a feeling - by strategically maneuvering around their emotions into serving her own ends.
5. Emotional immaturity. Believe me when I say this, pretty much all the accomplished men I’ve encountered display immature tendencies. I think it’s often because they’re the only ones who crave power, attention and control over other people and the environment to ever want to succeed under capitalist society. Most other healthy people often select themselves out of this petty game, seeing no higher purpose to it. Two of my immediate family members were General Managers at two very esteemed international companies, and I can safely say that life with them has been hell. All successful people have two faces. One they show to the outside world - as these amazing, charismatic, and intelligent people they are - and one they show to their families which I was able to witness first-hand - the immature, infantile and controlling side.
